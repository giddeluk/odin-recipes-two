Jollof Rice

Description

Nigerian Jollof Rice is a rich, vibrant one-pot rice dish known for its smoky depth and bold, savory flavor. The base of the dish is a smooth tomato-pepper reduction—typically made from blended red bell peppers (tatashe), tomatoes, onions, and hot habanero or scotch bonnet peppers (atarodo). Long-grain parboiled rice is simmered directly in this concentrated tomato sauce along with a seasoned meat stock (usually chicken or beef), curry powder, thyme, and bay leaves. As the rice cooks, it absorbs the intense savory flavors. A signature feature of authentic Nigerian Jollof is the slight burning or charring at the bottom of the pot (bottom pot), which infuses the entire dish with a characteristic, irresistible smoky aroma.

Ingredients

  • Long-grain parboiled rice
  • Blend base: Red bell peppers, fresh tomatoes, onions, and scotch bonnets
  • Tomato paste (for deep color and flavor)
  • Cooking oil
  • Meat stock (chicken or beef stock)
  • Seasonings: Curry powder, dried thyme, bay leaves, bouillon cubes, and salt

Steps

  1. Blend the base: Blend red bell peppers, tomatoes, onions, and scotch bonnet peppers until smooth.
  2. Boil down: Cook the blended mix in a pot until the excess water evaporates and it thickens.
  3. Fry the sauce: Heat oil, fry sliced onions, add tomato paste, and pour in the pepper mix to fry until oil floats to the top.
  4. Season: Stir in curry powder, thyme, bay leaves, salt, bouillon cubes, and rich meat stock.
  5. Add the rice: Wash long-grain parboiled rice thoroughly, add it to the pot, and mix well with the sauce.
  6. Seal and simmer: Cover the pot tightly with aluminum foil and a lid to trap steam, cooking on low heat until the rice is tender.
  7. Smokey finish: Turn up the heat for the last 3–5 minutes to let the bottom slightly char for that signature smoky aroma.
